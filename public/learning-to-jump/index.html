Level 1 · PPO, then a fix

Learning to Jump

Five playthroughs, pulled straight from the training checkpoints with no edits or speedup. The first four are a PPO agent slowly working out the level from reward alone, and it never gets much past one clear in two. The fifth throws out that whole approach, and that’s the one that works. The completion numbers next to each clip come from hundreds or thousands of separate runs, not just the one you’re watching.

05

Solved

no PPO this time, just direct imitation of a working expert

PPO’s ceiling wasn’t a training problem. This level needs a jump at one exact moment after landing, and for about fifteen ticks on either side of that moment, the agent’s view of the world barely changes. It has no way to feel time passing. So we gave it one feature it tracks on its own, ticks since it last touched ground, and had it copy a simple rule-based controller that already solves the level every time. No reward signal, no PPO. That was enough.

100% det, 97.2% stochastic
1,000-episode stochastic evaluation, plus 1 deterministic episode (the level and greedy play are both fully deterministic, so one run is conclusive)

PPO training, even with expert demonstrations mixed in the whole way through, topped out around a 49% success rate per attempt and wouldn’t budge. Making the policy more confident in its own decisions made it worse, which told us it had never found a genuinely confident answer, just something that worked often enough. The fix wasn’t a better training run. It was noticing the agent couldn’t sense the one thing this level needed it to sense, and giving it that.

Before we found that fix, taking the best of 4 attempts from the strongest PPO checkpoint got us to 92.4%, measured directly rather than estimated. That was a reasonable way to work around an unreliable single attempt. Stage 5 doesn’t need the workaround anymore.
